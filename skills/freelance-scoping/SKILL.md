# Scope projects clearly

> Scopes and statements of work that fix the outcome, the deliverables, the assumptions, and the exclusions before the work starts, so gaps never become arguments. Use when writing an estimate, a scope, or a statement of work.

**Track:** 💼 Freelance & Business · **Domain:** Winning Clients · **Level:** intermediate · **~35 min**

**Who this is for:** Freelancers, Consultants, Agencies, Independent Developers

## When to Use This Skill

Scopes and statements of work that fix the outcome, the deliverables, the assumptions, and the exclusions before the work starts, so gaps never become arguments. Use when writing an estimate, a scope, or a statement of work.
Use it whenever a matching task appears in conversation — the agent loads these instructions on demand.

## Steps

1. Define the outcome the client is buying in one sentence, then list only the deliverables that prove that outcome was delivered
2. Write the assumptions as explicit statements — the client provides the data by day three — so a miss becomes a known trigger instead of an argument
3. List the exclusions explicitly, because what is not included settles more disputes than what is
4. Attach a delivery calendar with dates, review windows, and what a client delay does to each downstream date
5. Name the change process in the document: how a request gets priced, who approves it, and how the timeline moves
6. Get the client to restate the scope in their own words in writing before you start, because silence here becomes a dispute later

## Common Pitfalls

- Scoping by listing tasks when the client is buying an outcome, then getting held to the task list
- Leaving client responsibilities out, so their delay surfaces later as your missed deadline

## Success Signals

- Every engagement starts with a written scope the client restated in their own words
- Zero projects started without documented exclusions
- Change requests priced and approved before work starts, not invoiced after
