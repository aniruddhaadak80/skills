# Write winning grant proposals

> Grant proposals that answer a funder's actual question, tie every budget line to an outcome, and survive the reviewer's budget screen. Use when responding to a foundation, corporate, or government funding opportunity.

**Track:** 🌍 Nonprofit & Impact · **Domain:** Fundraising & Governance · **Level:** advanced · **~40 min**

**Who this is for:** Nonprofit Leaders, Program Managers, Fundraisers, Board Members

## When to Use This Skill

Grant proposals that answer a funder's actual question, tie every budget line to an outcome, and survive the reviewer's budget screen. Use when responding to a foundation, corporate, or government funding opportunity.
Use it whenever a matching task appears in conversation — the agent loads these instructions on demand.

## Steps

1. Read the guidelines as a scoring sheet and map every requirement to a section before drafting a single sentence
2. Open with the problem in the funder's priority language, then your organisation's own evidence that you can solve it
3. Write outcomes as measurable change for the people served rather than activities delivered, because reviewers fund results
4. Build the budget line by line from the program design so every cost traces to a stated activity and the overhead rate matches your policy
5. Show the evaluation plan and the evidence you already hold, and state the gaps honestly, because reviewers find them anyway
6. Match the request to the funding guidance exactly and check every required attachment before submission, not after

## Common Pitfalls

- Reusing a generic proposal body that answers none of the requirements in the guidelines
- Padding the budget with unrequested line items until it gets cut and the whole request is scored down

## Success Signals

- Every funder requirement traced to a section before submission
- Budget lines reconciling to the narrative and to the overhead policy
- Zero missing attachments and a submission date hit with hours to spare
