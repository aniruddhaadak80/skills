# Write clear, enforceable HR policies

> HR policies written as numbered rules with a plain-language shadow, a worked scenario, and a scheduled review, so they stay lawful and get used. Use when drafting, rewriting, or auditing a people policy.

**Track:** 👥 People & HR · **Domain:** Policy & Engagement · **Level:** intermediate · **~35 min**

**Who this is for:** HR Managers, Recruiters, People Ops, Team Leads

## When to Use This Skill

HR policies written as numbered rules with a plain-language shadow, a worked scenario, and a scheduled review, so they stay lawful and get used. Use when drafting, rewriting, or auditing a people policy.
Use it whenever a matching task appears in conversation — the agent loads these instructions on demand.

## Steps

1. Start from the decision the policy has to settle; a manager reading it at 4pm on a Friday needs one clear answer, not a preamble
2. Write numbered rules and put a plain-language summary under each, because a legal clause nobody can apply is not a control
3. Attach a worked scenario to every rule: the situation, the correct action, and who makes the call
4. State the boundary explicitly, including what the policy does not cover and where escalation starts
5. Name the policy owner, the legal reviewer, and the date of the next review before publishing, not after someone asks
6. Publish where a manager can find it in one search and brief the team briefly, since an unread policy is a liability rather than a safeguard

## Common Pitfalls

- Drafting as a legal defence document: defensible in a tribunal, unreadable at a desk
- Publishing with no review date, so the policy quietly contradicts newer law and newer practice

## Success Signals

- Every policy carries a named owner and a review date
- Manager policy questions answered from the document without escalation
- Zero policies past their scheduled review date
