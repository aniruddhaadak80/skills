# Write plain-language government content

> Resident-facing forms, letters, and pages rewritten to plain-language conventions and tested with people who have never used the service. Use when publishing or rewriting any government content that residents read.

**Track:** 🏛️ Public Sector & GovTech · **Domain:** Digital Services · **Level:** intermediate · **~35 min**

**Who this is for:** Civic Technologists, Program Managers, Policy Analysts, Gov IT Teams

## When to Use This Skill

Resident-facing forms, letters, and pages rewritten to plain-language conventions and tested with people who have never used the service. Use when publishing or rewriting any government content that residents read.
Use it whenever a matching task appears in conversation — the agent loads these instructions on demand.

## Steps

1. State what the resident must do in the first sentence and what they need to have ready; front-load the action and the prerequisites
2. Cut the reading level to grade 8 with shorter sentences and common words, keeping jargon only where a statute requires the term
3. Turn instructions into headings that name the section, so a reader finds the line they need without reading the whole page
4. Put eligibility, deadlines, and costs in a table at the top, because buried conditions are where forms get abandoned
5. Test with a resident who has never used the service before and watch where they hesitate, then rewrite that spot first
6. Keep the legal requirement and the plain-language version in the same file and review them together, so the two cannot drift apart

## Common Pitfalls

- Simplifying the wording while quietly changing the legal meaning of the requirement
- Rewriting the web page but leaving the printed form, the letter template, and the SMS reminders in the old jargon

## Success Signals

- Resident-facing pages at grade 8 reading level or below
- Form abandonment measured before and after each rewrite
- Every rewrite signed off by both the content owner and the legal reviewer
